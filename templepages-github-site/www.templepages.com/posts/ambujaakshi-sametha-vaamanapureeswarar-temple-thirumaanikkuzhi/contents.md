Thirumaanikuzhi is 5 km away from the famous Vaishnava thalam Thiruvaheenthrapuram and 12 km from Cuddalore, on the Cuddalore–Panruti road. The Kedilam River flows to the north of the temple.

Maani means brahmachari. In the Vamana avatar, Lord Vishnu comes as a brahmachari and kills Mahabali. The third stride of Vamana sends Mahabali into a hole (kuri in Tamil). Though the act is divine, the method was not hailed as one. Vamana came and worshipped Lord Shiva here and was relieved. That is why this place is called Maanikuzhi, and Lord Shiva here is called Vamanapureeswarar.

This Lord also helped and saved a merchant named Athri from robbers. So he is called Uthavi Nayagar.

The temple welcomes us with a five-tiered Raja Gopuram and many sannadhis. On the way to the moolasthanam, there are beautiful sculptures depicting the story of the Vamana avatar.

The moolavar is always concealed by a screen on which a picture of Bhima Rudrar is drawn. There is a belief that the moolavar is with Ambal. Another belief is that he is being worshipped by Kuberan.

All the poojas are performed for Bhima Rudrar only. The screen is opened briefly only for deepa aaradhana. Also, because of this belief, there is no arthajama pooja in this temple.

There is also a separate temple for Ambal on the right side.

This sthalam is sung in the Thevaram and is more than 1,400 years old.

Sung by: Sambandar, Arunagirinadhar, Vallalar

Festivals: All the poojas and festivals are properly celebrated in this temple. One speciality is that Karthigai Deepa Dharisanam is celebrated on Rohini nakshatra on a hillock opposite the temple.

Pariharam: Those seeking the boon of a child pray to Ambikai Ambujakshi.

Other names of Swami: Vamanapureeswarar, Udavi Nayagar, Maanikkavaradar

Ambal: Ambujakshi, Udavi Nayagi, Maanikkavalli

Temple timings: Morning – 6:00 a.m. to 11:00 a.m.; evening – 4:30 p.m. to 8:30 p.m.
