
OVERVIEW

Praised in Tamil hymns as Thirucheeralaivaai, Thiruchendur is a coastal town in the Tuticorin district of Tamil Nadu. It houses the Sri Senthilandavar Temple, the second of the six sacred abodes of Lord Murugan (Arupadaiveedu), and is well-connected by trains and buses.

STHALAPURANAM (TEMPLE LEGEND)

* The Asura's Oppression: The demon Surapadma received boons from Lord Shiva, became arrogant, and enslaved the devas across all three worlds.
* Birth of Lord Muruga: To save the devas, Lord Shiva opened his third eye, creating six sparks of fire. These were transformed into babies at Saravana Poigai, suckled by the Kirithika nymphs, and united by Goddess Uma into Lord Arumuga (six faces and twelve arms).
* Surasamharam: Lord Muruga camped at Thiruchendur and sent Veerabahu as an emissary. When Surapadma refused to yield, a war ensued. On the sixth day, Lord Muruga pierced Surapadma—who had transformed into a mango tree—with his lance. The broken pieces turned into his mount (the peacock) and his banner (the cock).
* Worship of Shiva: Desiring to worship his father after the battle, Mayan (the divine architect) built this shrine. To this day, Lord Subramaniyan is seen in a worshipping posture in the sanctum sanctorum. This victory is celebrated grandly during the 6-day Kandha Sashti festival.

DUTCH OCCUPATION (1646–1648)
The Dutch East India Company occupied the temple during their war with the Portuguese. When forced by the Naik ruler to vacate in 1653, they stole the main deity's idol. Encountering a severe storm at sea, they dropped the idol into the water, and the storm immediately ceased. Later, Lord Senthil Aandavan appeared in a dream to Vadamaliyappa Pillai, who—along with sponsor Athitha Nadar—successfully retrieved the idol from the sea.

OTHER NOTABLE DETAILS

* Jayanthi Nathar: The presiding Shiva form inside the temple is called Jayanthi Nathar ("the god who gives victory").
* Naazhi Kinaru: A sacred freshwater well located just meters away from the salty ocean. Devotees traditionally bathe in the sea and then in this well before entering the temple.
* Significance: The temple is listed as a Thevara Vaipu Thalam and is a venerated Guru Sthalam where Lord Senthilandavar helps devotees overcome horoscope doshas related to a weak Jupiter (Guru).
