
Kodiyidaiyamman Sametha Maasilamaneeswarar Temple is in Thirumullaivayil, a western outskirt of Madras/Chennai between Ambattur and Avadi. In old Tamil text, this place is known as Vada-Thirumullaivayil, meaning northern Thirumullaivayil, to disambiguate from the same-named temple town near Sirkazhi in Thanjavur district.

Legend says that this place exists from Krutha Yugam itself.

In Krutha Yugam it existed as Rathnapuram, in Thretha Yugam as Vilva Vanam, in Dwapara Yugam as Shenbaga Vanam, and in Kaliyugam as Mullai Vanam.

This temple was built by King Thondaiman, and the story about the name is as follows:

<strong>Sthalapuranam</strong>

Once, there was a tribe known as Kurumbars who lived around this region. They were headed by two chiefs, Onan and Kaanthan. They were bandits who would loot and destroy places at will and take other people's belongings.

When they were done, they would go into their forts to hide, which were built so strong that nobody was able to capture them.

Their atrocities increased every day and the news reached Thondaiman, the ruling king. Thondaiman brought his army to fight the rogue bandits. Unable to withstand the disciplined army, Onan and Kaanthan retreated to their forts, performed a yagna to please their Lord Bhairava, and with his blessings brought a demon to fight the king.

The king was unprepared for this and faced defeat. When he retreated worriedly, his elephant's feet got stuck in a kind of jasmine creeper called mullai in Tamil. The elephant was unable to move. Seeing this, the king wielded his knife. Instead of the creeper being cut, he saw blood oozing out.

A surprised king got down from the elephant and removed the creeper to see a Shiva Lingam cut at the top with blood coming out of it.

Distressed with his own act, Thondaiman took the knife to kill himself, and that's when the Lord gave him darshan, told him that he would never be decapitated, that He is always pure and wholesome ("Maasu Illa Mani" in Tamil), and blessed him.

He told Nandi to go fight with the king. Seeing Nandi, the demon and the two asuras surrendered to the king, and thus he brought peace to the land. The Nandi is still seen facing east to mark the occasion.

Thondaiman, to celebrate the victory, brought two pillars made of the erukkam plant and installed them in the Artha Mandapam of the Lord. They can still be seen today.

The defeated Onan and Kaanthan later realized their sins, built a temple in Kanchipuram, and attained salvation. The temple is also sung about in Thevaram and is known as Ona Kandhan Thali.

Innumerous other people worshipped and attained salvation at this place. The list includes Indran, Indrani, Iravatham, Dhurvasa Rishi, Rama's sons Lava and Kusa, Sun, Vasishtar, Kamadhenu, Chandran, Ashwini Devas, Brugu Muni, Lord Karthikeya, and Sundharamoorthy Nayanmar.

Sundharar came here first after he lost his eyesight at Thiruvotriyur. He got one eye back later in Kanchipuram.

The temple is a large one with the typical Gajabrushta Vimana as seen in other temples in this area. There is a separate sannidhi for 'Rishaba Naayakar', the form the Lord took to give darshan to Thondaiman.

Legend says that the beautiful Goddess Kodiyidai Amman forms a trio with Vadivudai Amman of Thiruvotriyur and Thiruvudai Amman of Minjur, and should be worshipped on the same day to get tremendous benefits.

This temple is featured in Arunagirinathar's Thiruppugazh and also in Ramalinga Vallalar's Arutpa.
